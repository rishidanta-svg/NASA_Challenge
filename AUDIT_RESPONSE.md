# Response to the "POLTERGEIST physics model" audit

The audit was written about a different model ("POLTERGEIST"). Its numbers (7.19 m vs 7.16 m RMSE, 2.45 vs 2.58 m, ~52 m confidence ranges, a June 30 NISAR image) do not come from this repository's pipeline. Each point was therefore judged on whether it applies to **this** project: checking where each satellite puts a coastal **boundary** against tower lidar at Duck, NC. The team chose boundary position as the target.

All results referred to here are generated by code into `VALIDATION_SUMMARY.md` (section "Independent test") and the app (Sensors & trust page). This file deliberately repeats no numbers.

| # | Audit point | Applies here? | What was done |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | NISAR does not meaningfully influence results | **Yes** | `src/improvements.py` now learns each radar sensor's offset (NISAR's separately for each viewing direction) and noise level on training rows only, and combines sensors with inverse-variance weights, with and without NISAR, on identical rows. The result is reported whatever it shows. |
| 2 | Sentinel-2 and environmental data barely used | **Partly** | Feeding Sentinel-2 into the dune-toe prediction was **not** done: it would merge two different physical features, which the project rules forbid. Waves were added where physics says they matter: the visible wet/dry line sits at tide + wave runup. Data: FRF 17 m Waverider (Hs, Tp, QC flag 1 only). |
| 3 | Not physics-informed | **Yes, for the waterline** | Wave runup from Stockdon et al. (2006) R2% with published coefficients (nothing tuned), plus tide, moved along a planar beach slope. No neural network: there are far too few independent samples to train one honestly. |
| 4 | Predicts the wrong target | **No** | Boundary position was the team's chosen target. Elevation change is what lidar measures; the satellites are scored against it, and no satellite is claimed to measure elevation change. |
| 5 | Lidar leakage | **Yes** | The physics no longer uses the evaluation lidar: beach slopes come from **separate calibration scans** taken days before each evaluation scan. The independent test also holds out a **whole date and a 100 m stretch of beach** together, with all tuning and calibration done on other dates and places. A self-check recomputes every fold to prove it. One earlier finding (the tide-corrected Sentinel-2 retreat matching the contour) depended on the evaluation lidar's slope; it is now downgraded to NOT DEMONSTRATED in the summary, with the reason. |
| 6 | Simple baselines not compared | **Yes** | Every method is compared, on exactly the same rows, with "typical position" (the median of the training rows' lidar line) and, for June-to-October movement, with "no change". Tags use the same written rules as everything else. |
| 7 | Timing and location accuracy | **Partly** | This pipeline already used the June 18 NISAR pass and reports every image's time and water level. Added: a location-sensitivity test (edges shifted by 5 and 10 m), reported under "Where this could be wrong". |
| 8 | Unreliable ~52 m confidence ranges | **No** | Not this pipeline's number. Intervals here are 95% block-bootstrap intervals over 50 m stretches of beach, and the number of independent blocks is reported next to them. |

## What the independent test means for the pitch (read the tags in the summary)

- The strongest result is the waterline: Sentinel-2 corrected for tide (with a beach slope from an earlier survey) beats the "typical position" guess on every date. It needs the tide gauge and a prior beach survey, so it is **not satellite-only**.
- Wave runup physics did **not** improve on the tide correction at these dates, which all had small waves.
- For the dune toe, simply assuming its typical position beats every radar method, including the calibrated NISAR combination. Radar edges are near the dune, but here they do not tell you more about the dune toe than "it is about where it usually is".
- Adding NISAR to Sentinel-1 did **not** improve the dune line. NISAR's edge moves with viewing direction.
- So the honest message stays "different sensors see different features, and here is how far to trust each". Do not present the project as physics-informed fusion that accurately measures coastal change.
